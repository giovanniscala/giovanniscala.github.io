About

I am a theoretical physicist working at the interface of quantum information, quantum communication, quantum foundations, mathematical physics, and quantum optics. My research develops practical ways to certify and protect quantum resources under realistic constraints, including finite data, noise, loss, imperfect devices, and restricted measurement access.

Current themes include finite-size security in quantum key distribution, device-independent methods and self-testing, entanglement detection through randomized measurements, quantum error filtration, Bell nonlocality, and quantum machine learning. Earlier work spans positive maps and entanglement witnesses, light-matter interaction, and correlation plenoptic imaging.

From November 2026, I will join Nicolaus Copernicus University in Toruń as an Assistant Professor and Principal Investigator of the NCN SONATA 21 project Dynamical Resource for Multipartite Quantum Systems (grant 2025/59/D/ST2/02708; November 2026–October 2029).

Research Themes

Quantum correlations from foundations to applications.

*
Entanglement Certification
Positive maps, local invariants, and experimentally accessible randomized-measurement tests for detecting multipartite and high-dimensional entanglement.
Secure Quantum Communication
Finite-size security, device-independent quantum key distribution, and discrete- and continuous-variable protocols under realistic constraints.
Quantum Foundations and Self-Testing
Bell nonlocality, contextuality, operational nonclassicality, and certification with limited trust in devices.
Protection of Quantum Information
Error filtration, noise robustness, and resource-aware protocols for quantum information processing and sensing.
Quantum Optics and Imaging
Correlation plenoptic imaging, second-order interference, turbulence, and spatially extended light-matter interaction.
Quantum Machine Learning
Fidelity kernels, kernel concentration, finite-shot resource scaling, and learning quantum phase transitions.
